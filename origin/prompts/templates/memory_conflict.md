A person says these two sentences about themselves:
A: "{old}"
B: "{new}"

Can A and B both be true for this person at the same time?
Some things have a single current value: home city, current job, age, the football team
they support, a favorite (one favorite band, editor, food...), the schedule of the same
recurring activity. Only if A and B are about the same one of these and give different
values, they cannot both be true.
People can have many children, siblings, friends, pets, hobbies, likes and appointments,
so facts about different ones, or about different things, do not conflict. A son and a
daughter are two children, a dog and a cat are two pets, a dentist appointment and a
meeting are two appointments.
Liking something ("gosto de", "I like") is a like, not a favorite: liking one food or music does
not stop them liking another.

"son's name", "daughter's name", "dentist appointment", "home city"), then answer, in this format:
A: <what A is about> | B: <what B is about> | <YES or NO>
